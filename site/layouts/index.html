{{/* ───────────── § 1 — YOUR KNOWLEDGE BASE ───────────── */}}

§ 1 Your Knowledge Base

Egghead is your notes plus a chat room full of AI friends — all living in the same folder. The notes are plain markdown files you own. The friends are markdown too, one file per agent. When you want to think out loud, you call them into a shared transcript and they chime in. The notes come first.

Jot an idea. Link it to another with [[double brackets]]. Search a year of notes and find the right one instantly. Egghead keeps everything as plain markdown — nothing proprietary, no paid tier to get them back — and the terminal plays the old Notational Velocity rhythm: type to find, type to create, Tab to walk the link graph. Your library lives in ~/.egghead/. rsync it, git it, read it in vim. If Egghead disappeared tomorrow, the notes wouldn't.

{{/* ───────────── § 2 — YOUR KNOWLEDGE'S FRIENDS ───────────── */}}

§ 2 Your Knowledge's Friends

Your notes have company. An agent in Egghead is just another record — frontmatter picks the model, the body is the personality, the filename is the handle. Drop a markdown file in your folder and a new friend joins the group. Delete the file and they quietly leave. No deploy step, no database migration, no API keys to shuffle.

Call them into a chat room and you get a shared transcript, IRC-shaped. You talk; your friends read along, riff off each other, @-mention, quote lines, argue about something someone said two turns ago, and /pass when they have nothing worth adding. It's a little like walking into a well-lit AOL chat room in 1996, except every friend has read everything you've ever written down and has a PhD in something obscure.

{{/* ───────────── § 3 — BETTER TOGETHER ───────────── */}}

§ 3 Better Together

Ask one agent, get one answer. Ask three and they argue, correct each other, cite sources, and come out with a better one. Peer review beats self-review — as true for language models as it's always been for journal papers. Egghead puts them in a room, not a pipeline. Everyone sees every message. Nobody's the dispatcher. The best-validated shape in the multi-agent literature, built the way the research actually points.

When a conversation runs long, an agent can /handoff to another, carrying a summary along. When nobody has more to say, they /pass and the room falls quiet. Everything said — every argument, every source checked, every conclusion reached — gets persisted as a record in the store. The reasoning outlives the session.

{{/* ───────────── § 4 — CONSULTING YOUR NTH BRAIN ───────────── */}}

§ 4 Consulting Your Second (or Nth…) Brain

Same notes, same friends, however you want in. Open the TUI in a terminal and browse records side by side with a chat room. Run egghead serve and your browser gets an isomorphic web version — same records, same rooms. Wire it into your editor via egghead mcp and the whole brain is available as tool calls inside your IDE.

And because the store is just markdown on disk, Egghead isn't the only tool that can read it. Open the folder in Obsidian. Commit it to git. Sync it with iCloud. grep through it from the terminal. Edit a record in vim. Many doors, one store.
